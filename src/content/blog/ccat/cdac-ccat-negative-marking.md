## CDAC C-CAT Negative Marking

One of the biggest mistakes candidates make in the CDAC C-CAT exam is attempting every question without considering the negative marking.

Since every incorrect answer reduces your score, understanding **when to attempt a question and when to skip it** can significantly improve your final rank.

Fortunately, the marking scheme is simple and remains the same for every section.

---

## Official Marking Scheme

According to the official C-DAC admission booklet:

| Response | Marks |
|---|---:|
| Correct Answer | **+3** |
| Incorrect Answer | **−1** |
| Unattempted Question | **0** |

This marking scheme applies to **all three sections (A, B and C)** of the examination.

---

## Why Negative Marking Matters

Suppose you answer:

- 40 questions correctly
- 10 questions incorrectly

Your score will be:

- Correct answers: **40 × 3 = 120**
- Wrong answers: **10 × (−1) = −10**

**Final Score = 110**

Those 10 incorrect guesses cost you **10 marks**, which can make a noticeable difference in your rank, especially when competition is high.

---

## Should You Guess?

The answer depends on how confident you are.

### ✅ Attempt the Question If:

- You can eliminate two or more options.
- You are reasonably confident about the concept.
- It's an educated guess rather than a random guess.
- You have solved similar questions during practice.

---

### ❌ Skip the Question If:

- You have absolutely no idea.
- All options look equally possible.
- You are guessing purely by chance.
- Spending more time will reduce your performance on easier questions.

Random guessing usually hurts more than it helps.

---

## A Simple Decision Rule

Ask yourself these questions before marking an answer:

1. Do I understand what the question is asking?
2. Can I eliminate at least one or two options?
3. Do I remember a related concept?

If the answer is **yes**, attempting the question may be worthwhile.

If the answer is **no**, moving on is often the better choice.

---

## Section-wise Impact

### Section A

Questions in English, Aptitude and Reasoning are generally quicker to solve.

Avoid overthinking difficult aptitude problems that consume too much time.

---

### Section B

This section carries the greatest importance for most software-related courses.

Many questions are theory-based, while others involve code snippets.

Attempt questions where you understand the concept, even if you're not 100% certain of the final answer.

---

### Section C

Candidates applying for electronics programmes should follow the same strategy.

Questions on Digital Electronics, Computer Architecture and Microprocessors often test conceptual understanding rather than lengthy calculations.

---

## Time Management and Guessing

A common mistake is spending several minutes on a single difficult question.

Instead:

- Solve easy questions first.
- Mark difficult ones for review (if the exam interface allows).
- Return only if time remains.
- Never rush through the last few minutes making random guesses.

A calm and disciplined approach usually scores better than attempting every question.

---

## Practice With Negative Marking

When solving mock tests or previous C-CAT papers:

- Use the official **+3 / −1** marking scheme.
- Calculate your score after every test.
- Analyse why each incorrect answer happened.
- Focus on reducing mistakes instead of only increasing attempts.

Improving your accuracy by just a few questions can have a bigger impact than attempting additional uncertain questions.

---

## Common Myth

**"Attempt every question because there is a chance of getting it right."**

This strategy is risky.

With four options, a completely random guess has only a **25% chance** of being correct. Over many questions, random guessing is unlikely to improve your overall score because incorrect answers accumulate negative marks.

Educated guesses based on partial knowledge are very different from blind guesses.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The official marking scheme is **+3 for every correct answer** and **−1 for every incorrect answer**.
- Unattempted questions receive **0 marks**.
- Avoid blind guessing.
- Attempt questions only when you can eliminate options or apply relevant concepts.
- Practising with the official marking scheme helps you develop better exam judgement.
- Accuracy is often more valuable than attempting every question.
