## My AIR 35 Story

Before this site existed, it was just my own revision notes.

I came back to preparing for a technical entrance exam after a couple of years away from active coding — I'd spent that time attempting a different competitive exam and hadn't cleared it, so I decided to go back into IT and target CDAC C-CAT instead for the PG-DAC course, which meant clearing Section A and Section B.

The English and aptitude side of Section A wasn't new to me — I'd already covered similar ground during my earlier prep — so I didn't spend much time there. Almost all of my one month of preparation went into the Section B core CS syllabus: C programming, data structures, OOP, operating systems, and networking.

The biggest realization from that month: **you don't need to go deep on every single topic**. C-CAT rewards a strong, even base across the whole syllabus far more than expert-level depth on two or three favorite subjects. A candidate who can reliably answer 80% of every topic will always outscore one who can answer 100% of half the topics and guess on the rest — especially with negative marking working against blind guesses.

I built my own material from scattered sources because nothing consolidated existed in one place. That gap is the entire reason this site exists now. I ended up ranking **AIR 35**, which was enough to get into a top CDAC centre of choice.

[Read the original write-up →](https://medium.com/@anand98/how-i-secured-air-35-in-the-cdac-c-cat-exam-56e74bce9e0a)

Everything below is the consolidated version of what I wish I'd had in one place back then.

## What is CDAC C-CAT

CDAC C-CAT (Common Admission Test) is the entrance exam conducted by the **Centre for Development of Advanced Computing** for admission to its PG diploma programmes — PG-DAC, PG-DBDA, PG-DAI, PG-DESD, PG-DVLSI, PG-DIoT, PG-DITISS, and PG-DCSF among others. Your C-CAT rank, combined with your course and centre preferences, decides your seat during counselling.

## Complete Syllabus, Section by Section

C-CAT is split into three independent sections, each with its own time limit — time doesn't carry over between sections. Which sections you attempt depends on your course category.

| Section | Broad Coverage | Who Attempts It |
|---|---|---|
| Section A | English, Quantitative Aptitude, Reasoning, Computer Fundamentals | Everyone |
| Section B | C Programming, Data Structures, OOP/C++, Operating Systems, Computer Networks, DBMS | Category II & III |
| Section C | Electronics, Digital Logic, Microprocessors | Category III (VLSI, Embedded, IoT courses) |

## How to Prepare — A Realistic Strategy

This is the phase-wise version of the "even base, not narrow depth" approach from my own prep.

### Phase 1 — Diagnose (Days 1-3)

Attempt one section-wise mock or a previous year paper cold, no revision first. This tells you which of the ~20 topics across Section A/B are already fine and which need real time — don't skip this step and start "studying everything from scratch."

→ **[CDAC C-CAT Previous Year Question Papers](/ccat/blog/cdac-ccat-previous-year-papers/)**

### Phase 2 — Build the Base (bulk of your timeline)

Go topic by topic through the cheat sheets, in this order for most candidates: C Programming → Data Structures → OOP/C++ → Operating Systems → Networks → DBMS → Quant/Reasoning/English. Read the cheat sheet first, then the full topic guide if a concept doesn't click, then the MCQs for that topic immediately after — don't batch all the reading before any practice.

→ **[CDAC C-CAT Preparation Strategy](/ccat/blog/cdac-ccat-preparation-strategy/)**

### Phase 3 — Drill Weak Topics (final 2 weeks)

Flashcards for fast active recall on anything you keep getting wrong, timed MCQ sets on the specific topics that are still shaky — not full mocks yet.

→ **[Flashcards](/flashcards/)**

Full timed, section-wise mocks under real exam conditions, review every wrong answer the same day, not the week after.

## How to Use the Cheat Sheets, Flashcards and MCQs on This Site

**Cheat sheets** are one page per topic — formulas, syntax, definitions. Use them for a 2-minute scan before an MCQ set, or the night before the exam. Not for first-time learning.

→ **[Browse all cheat sheets](/ccat/cheat-sheets//)**

**Flashcards** are flip-card active recall drills. Use them *after* you've read a topic guide once — flashcards test retention, they don't teach a concept from zero.

→ **[Start flipping](/flashcards/)**

**MCQs** are topic-wise, with instant feedback. Use them immediately after reading a topic (to check understanding) and again in the final week under a timer (to simulate exam pressure).

→ **[Start practicing](/mcqs/)**

**Blog / topic guides** are the deepest layer — full explanations, common mistakes, interview questions, and solved MCQs for a single topic. Use these when a cheat sheet line doesn't make sense on its own.

→ **[Browse the blog](/ccat/blog/)**
